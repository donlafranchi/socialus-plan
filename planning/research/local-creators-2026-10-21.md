# Local scouts: members who find and showcase local businesses (2026-10-21)

**As of 2026-10-21** · Source: hand-written research, product lane · Path: new territory (money, member trust, disclosure law) · Research only; nothing here is ruled.

**Bottom line: let members go out, find the local businesses nobody can find online, and post them on SocialUs first, then everywhere else. Reward them with credit, first-found standing and labelled perks, not cash, for the pilot. A four-week pilot with 3–5 local scouts and the dark-site businesses needs about 25–35 hours of build. The one call: what scouts get (A/B/C in § 5; recommended A, no cash yet).**

*The PM, 2026-10-21, verbatim:* "So, what I'm thinking of is an outreach program to these businesses. this is a side project that we an hopefully market to these businesses if they still operate. Next we should be using other models like TikToks influencers who go out and "review" businesses to gain followers and try to create a pattern like that for our platform. Local influencers going out and finding these businesses to help them get found and to help them promote. this would be a new system that would need to be developed. We essentially want to copy all the other apps high value features in a way that helps our app. pretend for example that these "influencers" go out and create for TikTok as they normally do but we allow them to post on our site prior to them posting on TikTok or elsewhere and incentivize them somehow to do so. In essence copy from other apps to help our communities and figure out how to help influencers do that. I use "influencers" as a shorthand here and we'd call them something different so you do the same."

"These businesses" are the dark-site local businesses (farms, ranches, bakeries with blank, stale or no websites) being triaged for the outreach pilot (`planning/research/dark-site-outreach-2026-10-21.md`, when it lands). This doc calls the people **scouts** (§ 3).

## 1. What other apps do

**Three things work everywhere: trust that the reviewer pays their own way, cheap status that renews, and pay only when something real happens.** Cash funds from big platforms come and go, and the cases that went wrong are all undisclosed pay, fake reviews, or creators taking from small businesses.

**What goes wrong, and the law:**
- **Undisclosed sponsorship.** The FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR 255, revised 2023) require any material connection to be disclosed clearly and conspicuously, and free meals, discounts, affiliate pay and early access all count. A tag or repost can be an endorsement, and the platform or agency arranging it can be liable too ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/attachments/press-releases/ftc-staff-reminds-influencers-brands-clearly-disclose-relationship/influencer_template.pdf)).
- **Fake reviews.** The FTC rule (16 CFR 465, effective 2024-10-21) bans fake or AI reviews, buying positive or negative reviews, undisclosed insider reviews and fake follower counts, at up to $51,744 per violation ([Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/the-ftc-cracks-down-on-fake-reviews-with-new-rule), [Manatt](https://manatt.com/insights/newsletters/client-alert/ftc-issues-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-and-tes)).
- **Pay-for-play.** A 2025 Phoenix bakery case exposed paid food coverage passed off as opinion ([KJZZ](https://www.kjzz.org/the-show/2025-11-03/jl-patisseries-influencer-saga-reveals-pay-to-play-side-of-food-criticism)).
- **Extracting from small businesses.** Influencers asking for free meals in exchange for posts draw regular public call-outs, e.g. Dublin 2021, a meal for four requested and then a no-show ([Irish Post](https://www.irishpost.com/news/dublin-restaurant-slams-social-media-influencer-for-demanding-free-meal-for-four-then-failing-to-show-up-203350)). The fix that works is Keith Lee's: pay, then tip.

## 2. The SocialUs version

**A scout is a member who finds a local business, visits, and posts a showcase here first. After the first-run window they cross-post to TikTok or Instagram with a "first on SocialUs" link.** The showcase lands on the business's Page, so a farm with no website gets photos and a story it never had to make. Nothing a scout does moves a Page up in Find.

<details><summary>Details</summary>

**The loop:**
1. A member joins the Scouts Group. Groups are joined, never assigned, so being a scout is derived from a dated membership, never a stored role (the same pattern `product/systems/creator.md` uses for selling).
2. They pick a business, often an unclaimed dark-site Page from the outreach list, or one they love that has no Page yet, and visit as a paying customer.
3. They post a **showcase** on SocialUs: photos, a short write-up, later video. It names the business's Page, and the business's Page shows it.
4. The showcase says **First on SocialUs · date**. After the window (§ 5, recommended 48 hours) the scout posts on TikTok or Instagram as they normally would, with the share kit: a link back to the Page, a caption line, and a QR card the scout can hand the owner.
5. The owner scans the QR card and claims the Page. The scout is credited as the one who brought them on.

**Fits our rulings:**
- **Works for the member** (`#pitch`): showcases reach a member through what they follow and save, and the scout is a member doing something they already enjoy.
- **No pay-to-rank on Find** [for now, Find only]: a showcase never reorders Find, and no scout or business pays to be shown. On Discover, after beta, showcases are the native, full-screen local content Discover needs to start with. Earned attention surfacing there is the 2026-09-12 "peer pressure for good" mechanism, not a loophole.
- **Honest origin, no badge for everything** (`cap-8`): every showcase carries one disclosure line the scout picks before posting: *Paid my own way* · *Comped* · *Gift or discount* · *I know the owner* · *Paid to post*. This is the FTC "clear and conspicuous" rule built into the composer, like TikTok's and Instagram's paid-partnership toggles. A "scout" mark on a person is not a rank, but the removed "Active in the community" badge (2026-09-07) is the warning: show *who first showcased this Page*, a fact on the Page, not a badge on the person.
- **Not a review.** No stars or scores; no review noun exists and none is added here. A showcase is a post about a business, so the FTC fake-review rule's main exposure (bought ratings) does not arise. Insider posts (an owner's friend) are the remaining risk, and the *I know the owner* line is there for that. It is, though, the first time a member writes about a Page they don't run, the third-party surface `IMAGINE.md` ("Discovery that knows what a person is optimizing for") says the product has never had; it rides the same report path.
- **Get out of the way:** self-serve join, self-serve post. Moderation is the existing report path (F078): a business or member reports a showcase like any other post. Scouts who keep breaking the code below leave the Group.
- **Local means money comes back:** a scout showcases a business whose money returns to the community, so a showcase is for a Page that can carry the Locally Owned claim, not a chain.
- **Members are the investors:** any money to a scout goes to a member, which the model allows. *How* (from transaction income, the community fund, or a referral credit) is a question for `socialus-legal`, never a PM decision (SETTLED banner).

**Scout code (one screen, at join):** pay your own way or say you didn't; never ask a business for anything in exchange for a post; never charge a business; label every showcase; no showcase of a business you own. The Keith Lee pattern, written down.

**Who gets what:**

| | Gets | Gives |
|---|---|---|
| **Business** | Photos and a story it didn't have to make; a Page claimed in person rather than by cold email; reach on TikTok and Instagram it never paid for; no fee to anyone | Nothing required. May offer a members' deal or a comp, which is then labelled |
| **Scout** | Credit on every Page they bring on ("first showcased by"); the first-run spot, their post is the one people find first; perks businesses choose to offer, labelled; later, a referral credit if counsel clears it (§ 5); a showcase reel that is portable and theirs | Visits, photos, honesty |
| **Members** | Places they could never have found by search; content that is local and labelled; Discover that is not empty on day one | Nothing |

**Feeds dark-site outreach:** scouts are the in-person channel email can't reach. A ranch with no website rarely answers a cold message, but a neighbour who bought eggs and handed over a QR card is a different conversation. The outreach list tells scouts where to go; scouts tell the list who is still operating.

**Feeds Discover:** Discover needs short, real, local video and photos; showcases are exactly that, with origin and disclosure already attached. It ties to the open short-video choice in `IMAGINE.md` (link or embed vs host natively): **a first-run window only works if we host the content**, so scouts are the strongest reason yet for hosting photos (and later video) natively.

</details>

## 3. What to call them

**Recommended: Scouts.** It says the action (going out to find what others haven't), fits dark-site businesses exactly, and no big local app uses it. "Creator" is out: it is already our word for a member who sells, hosts or organizes.

<details><summary>Details</summary>

| Name | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| **Scouts** (recommended) | Active: going out and finding. "Talent scout", "location scout". Unused by Google, Yelp or TikTok | Scouting America's names are its own; plain "Scouts" in a product is common, but check with counsel before it goes on a logo |
| Finders | Plain and warm | Collides with **Find**, the internal name for the tool side |
| Guides | Familiar | Google Local Guides owns it in people's heads |
| Locals | Warm | "Local" is ruled to mean money comes back, not a person; a second meaning confuses it |
| Neighbors | Fits our tone | Too broad: every member is a neighbour, and Nextdoor owns it |
| Regulars | Honest: they go back | Implies a customer of one place, not a finder of many |
| Spotters | Light, fun | Sounds like a gym or birdwatching |
| Trailblazers | Big | Too grand; reads as a badge |

The member-facing name is copy, so it is the PM's to approve. "Scouts" is used through this doc as the working name.

</details>

## 4. The smallest test

**Four weeks, 3–5 local scouts, the dark-site list. Build only what a scout needs to post first and say so: a Scouts Group, a showcase that names a Page, a first-on-SocialUs date, a disclosure line, and a share kit. About 25–35 hours.** It succeeds if scouts bring businesses on that email alone could not.

<details><summary>Details</summary>

**Shape:** recruit 3–5 people in the pilot area who already post local food or farm content (any follower count; the pilot tests the loop, not reach). Each takes 4–6 businesses from the dark-site list. About 20 businesses get a scout visit; the rest get the email outreach only, which makes a comparison.

**Build (code lane; estimates, not a ticket):**

| Piece | What | Hours |
|---|---|---|
| Scouts Group | An ordinary Group with the scout code as its description; no new role | 0.5 |
| Showcase names a Page | A post from the scout's own Page names another Page; the named Page shows it under "Showcased by members" | 8–12 |
| First on SocialUs | The post's own date, shown as "First on SocialUs"; an optional field for the scout to paste their TikTok or Instagram link later | 3–4 |
| Disclosure line | A required pick from five lines on any showcase, shown on the post | 4–6 |
| Share kit | A share link with a preview card, a caption line, and a printable QR card that opens the Page's claim | 5–7 |
| First showcased by | One line on a Page: who first showcased it, and when | 2–3 |
| Measures | Queries for the numbers below | 2–3 |
| **Total** | | **25–35** |

Not built for the pilot: video (photos first, pending the short-video ruling), any payment, any scout page or profile beyond the member's own, leaderboards.

**Measure:**
- **Claims:** share of scout-visited dark-site businesses that claim their Page in four weeks, against email-only. The headline number.
- **Still operating:** businesses scouts confirmed open or closed (feeds the outreach list).
- **Showcases** per scout per week, and how many scouts are still posting in week four.
- **First-run kept:** the gap between the SocialUs post and the pasted TikTok or Instagram link.
- **Came from outside:** visits and new members arriving through share links.
- **Follows** of showcased Pages within two weeks.
- **Honesty:** share of showcases labelled anything other than *Paid my own way*; reports on showcases; any business complaint.
- **Owner view:** one question to each claiming owner: "Was the scout's visit welcome?"

**Kill or keep:** keep if scout-visited businesses claim at least twice as often as email-only and no business complains about a scout asking for something. Otherwise stop and write down why.

</details>

## 5. Decisions for the PM

**Top call: what scouts get. Recommended A: credit, first-run standing and labelled business perks, no money in the pilot.** Money touches the member-payout question, which goes to counsel first; the pilot can show whether scouts come without it.

<details><summary>Details</summary>

**1. What scouts get (the top call)**
- **A) Recognition and perks, no money (recommended).** "First showcased by" credit, the first-run spot, and perks a business chooses to offer, always labelled. Yelp Elite and Google Local Guides run on status alone. Cheapest, no legal exposure, and it tests whether the loop works before money changes who shows up.
- B) A referral credit per business that claims its Page, paid from transaction income. DoorDash and Amazon pay only on a real result, and this would too. Needs counsel on member payouts first, and money draws scouts who chase claims, not stories.
- C) A share of the community give-back for scouts. Fits "money comes back", but the community decides give-back, and scouts taking a cut of it pits them against the community they serve.

**2. First-run window**
- A) 24 hours: Patreon's minimum; easy for scouts.
- **B) 48 hours, a scout may choose longer (recommended).** Long enough that the first people to see it are here; short enough that a scout loses nothing on TikTok.
- C) 7 days: a real head start, but scouts will skip it and post to TikTok first.

**3. Exclusivity**
- **A) None after the window (recommended).** First here, then anywhere. Patreon's early access still lets work go public; locking content in costs scouts their audience.
- B) Exclusive in return for a perk. More reason to come here, but it makes the perk a material connection that needs a label on every post.
- C) Some content only here (e.g. members-only extras). Later, if Discover wants it.

**4. Disclosure**
- **A) A required pick on every showcase, including "Paid my own way" (recommended).** No post can skip it, and the default is never "nothing to say". The FTC test is clear and conspicuous; a required line is.
- B) An optional toggle (TikTok's and Instagram's way). Lighter, but silence reads as independent when it may not be.
- C) Required, plus the platform checks a sample. Better evidence, but it puts staff in the middle; the report path already covers it.

**5. Name.** Scouts (§ 3).

[open-question owner=pm raised=2026-10-21] Which of A/B/C for what scouts get, the first-run window, exclusivity and disclosure; and whether "Scouts" is the name.

**Risks to watch:**
- A scout asks a business for free food. The code forbids it and the report path catches it; one case in the pilot is a stop-and-review.
- Impersonation: someone poses as a SocialUs scout to charge businesses (the Keith Lee scam). The share kit says in plain words that scouts never charge, and the QR card only opens the claim page.
- A scout posts about a friend's business without saying so. The *I know the owner* line; reports.
- Unclaimed Page photos (already an accepted risk, review 2026-11-30): scout photos are the scout's own, which helps.
- Scouts mean more posts to moderate, and later video into the child-safety scan.

</details>
