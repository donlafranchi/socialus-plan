# Wonders playbook

**A Wonder turns "does anyone else want this?" into a project neighbours run themselves: people sign up, take paid roles as small contracts, and the price of the finished thing covers every fee at cost, with no profit layer.** SocialUs provides the playbook, templates and a place to organize; members do the work and get paid for it.

**Your one call:** what goes in the first version (A, B or C, at the bottom). Recommend **B**.

*The PM, 2026-10-25:* "this should be a way for people to organize and accomplish something … offer their services for a fee like a contract and if things work out then the cost to the end user for the item of clothing covers the fees for each person that contributed."

**Worked example throughout:** a clothing co-op for men about 5'10" and 170–190 lb, buying straight from a factory in runs of 10,000–100,000 per item. The PM proposed it and won't run it; the Wonder asks who will lead and who will design.

## The seven stages

**Money moves only from Commit on; until payments exist, a Wonder stops at Organize** with interest sign-ups only.

<details><summary>Details</summary>

| Stage | What happens | Done when | Co-op example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Wonder** | A member posts the ask | Posted | "Clothes that fit men about 5'10", 170–190 lb, bought in bulk at cost. Who's in? Who'll lead? Who'll design?" |
| **2. Gather** | People say "I'm in" (with size) and "I can help as …" | Interest passes the threshold the poster set, and someone takes the lead | 2,000 "I'm in" with sizes; a lead steps up |
| **3. Organize** | The lead fills roles, sets the budget and price, picks the factory, runs samples | Every role is filled, the budget is open, a sample is approved | Designer and pattern-maker contracted; three factories quoted; sample round two approved |
| **4. Commit** | All-or-nothing pre-orders with a goal and a deadline; pledges are held, not charged | The goal is hit: everyone is charged. Missed: no one is | 10,000 shirts pledged in 30 days |
| **5. Make** | The factory produces; quality checks at milestones | Goods pass inspection | Pre-production sample, mid-run and final inspection |
| **6. Deliver** | Shipping, fulfilment, exchanges for wrong sizes | Every buyer has theirs | Freight to one warehouse, then parcels or local pickup |
| **7. Wrap-up** | Final accounts published, contributors paid in full, the reserve settled | Books closed; any surplus goes back to buyers or the community fund (buyers vote) | $6,000 of the $9,500 reserve unused: $0.60 back to each buyer |

</details>

## Roles are small paid contracts

**Each role is one contract: scope, fee, deliverable, deadline, and payment at milestones.** Anyone can do more than one role; a role can be unpaid if the person wants.

<details><summary>Details</summary>

Fees are illustrative for one shirt run of 10,000. The lead proposes them in the budget; buyers see them before they pledge.

| Role | Scope | Fee | Deliverable | Paid at |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lead** | Runs the project, fills roles, posts weekly updates | $8,000 | The project on time, updates every week | 25% Organize done · 50% goods shipped · 25% Wrap-up |
| **Designer** | The design and tech pack | $6,000 | Tech pack and artwork, licensed to the project | 50% tech pack · 50% sample approved |
| **Pattern-maker / sampler** | Graded patterns across sizes, sample rounds | $4,000 + samples at cost | Patterns, two sample rounds | Per round |
| **Sourcing** | Finds factories and fabric, collects quotes | $3,000 | Three comparable quotes, a recommendation | Quotes in · factory signed |
| **Quality** | Inspections before, during and after the run | $4,000 | Three inspection reports with photos | Per inspection |
| **Logistics** | Freight, customs, warehouse, parcels or pickup | $5,000 | Every order delivered, tracked | Goods landed · all delivered |
| **Support** | Answers buyers, handles size exchanges | $3,000 | Replies within two days | Monthly |
| **Treasurer** | Keeps the open books, reconciles, publishes the final accounts | $2,000 | Monthly statement, final accounts | Monthly · Wrap-up |

Total contributor fees: $35,000. A contributor who misses a deadline gets one written extension; after that the lead can re-contract the remaining work and pay only for what was delivered.

</details>

## Open-book pricing

**Item price = (materials + factory + contributor fees + shipping) ÷ units + a small reserve.** Every buyer sees every line before pledging. There is no profit layer; the reserve covers surprises and comes back if unused.

<details><summary>Details</summary>

| Line | At 10,000 units | At 100,000 units |
|---|---|---|
| Fabric and trims | $6.00 | $5.20 |
| Factory (cut, make, trim) | $5.00 | $4.10 |
| Contributor fees ($35k, then $90k at the bigger run) | $3.50 | $0.90 |
| Freight, duty, fulfilment | $4.00 | $3.40 |
| Payment processing (~3%) | $0.60 | $0.45 |
| Reserve (5%) | $0.95 | $0.70 |
| **Price per shirt** | **$20.05** | **$14.75** |

- The goal is the unit count where the price holds; below it the Wonder doesn't charge anyone.
- Bigger runs lower the price; a second tier ("if we reach 25,000, everyone pays $17") is shown upfront and applied to everyone.
- Any SocialUs transaction fee is a visible line, set in the income plan, never hidden in the price.
- Never extractive: no line pays anyone for doing nothing, and buyers see who earns what.

</details>

## Templates SocialUs provides

**Six fill-in-the-blank templates, so nobody starts from a blank page or pays a lawyer of their own.**

<details><summary>Details</summary>

1. **Wonder post:** what you want, who it's for, the threshold to move on, the roles you need, and "I'm in (pick your size)".
2. **Role contract:** scope, fee, deliverable, deadline, milestone payments, what happens on a missed deadline, and an **IP licence**: the designer keeps their copyright and grants the project a licence to make and sell the design for this Wonder and its reruns, credited by name. Plain words, vetted once at the platform level.
3. **Budget sheet:** the open-book lines above, filled live as quotes come in, with the price recalculated.
4. **Factory request for quote:** tech pack attached, units at each tier, fabric, sizes, sample rounds, lead time, payment terms, inspection access, minimum order.
5. **Size survey:** height, weight, chest, waist, sleeve, preferred fit, one garment that fits well today. It feeds the size split for the run (the co-op's 5'10", 170–190 lb range likely needs three sizes, not one).
6. **Launch checklist:** every role filled, sample approved, budget closed, price and goal set, deadline set, updates schedule set, exchange policy written, then open pre-orders.

</details>

## Pledges held, charged only at the goal

**Nobody pays unless the goal is hit, and contributors are paid at milestones from the collected money, never upfront from buyers' cards.**

<details><summary>Details</summary>

- Pledges are authorised and held until the deadline; the goal hit, all are charged together; missed, all are released.
- Money sits with the payment partner, not SocialUs (the platform is the technology layer, never the bank).
- The treasurer releases each milestone payment when its deliverable is marked done by the lead and seen by buyers in an update.
- Sample costs before Commit are the one gap: the lead can front them, a contributor can, or the Wonder runs a small "sample fund" pledge first.

</details>

## Trust: assume the best, with mechanisms

**We expect people to do what they said; the playbook makes it easy to see that they did, and simple to sort out when they didn't.**

<details><summary>Details</summary>

- **Progress updates:** the lead posts weekly with photos; a missed week shows on the Wonder.
- **Vouches:** members who've worked with a contributor can vouch for them; past Wonders a person delivered show on their profile.
- **Open books:** every payment out appears on the budget sheet the day it's made.
- **Dispute path:** talk first (a buyer or contributor raises it on the Wonder); then a mediator from past Wonder leads; then the contract's own terms (refund the undelivered part, re-contract the role). A person steps in only when the parties can't settle it.
- **Buyer protection:** if the project stops before Make, unspent money goes back to buyers pro rata.

</details>

## Guides on tap and past Wonders

**Short how-to guides for the hard parts, and every finished Wonder becomes a worked example the next one copies.**

<details><summary>Details</summary>

- **Finding a factory:** marketplaces and directories, what to ask, minimums, red flags, visiting or hiring an inspector.
- **Sample rounds:** what a tech pack is, how many rounds to expect (usually two or three), how to give fit feedback.
- **Sizing:** turning the size survey into a size split, grading, the cost of adding a size.
- **Shipping:** freight vs air, duty, one warehouse vs local pickup, exchanges.
- **Past Wonders:** each closed Wonder keeps its budget, timeline, contracts (names optional) and what went wrong, readable by anyone starting a new one. The co-op becomes the first.

</details>

## Precedents

**Every piece here is a well-worn path: we combine them, at cost, for neighbours.**

<details><summary>Details</summary>

- **Kickstarter Creator Handbook** (kickstarter.com/help/handbook): all-or-nothing funding, budgeting with fees and shipping, the update rhythm, and the advice to have a prototype before launch.
- **Massdrop / Drop** (drop.com): group buys where the price falls as more people join, and community-designed products made with manufacturers.
- **Open Collective** (opencollective.com, docs.opencollective.com): every dollar in and out public; a fiscal host holds money for groups that aren't a legal entity of their own.
- **US Federation of Worker Cooperatives** (usworker.coop) and the **Democracy at Work Institute** (institute.coop): co-op development guides, member ownership, surplus returned by patronage.
- **Gitcoin bounties** (gitcoin.co): work broken into scoped, priced tasks paid on delivery.

</details>

## What it depends on

**Money waits for the legal entity and a payment provider that can hold pledges; until then a Wonder runs Wonder → Gather → Organize with interest sign-ups only.**

<details><summary>Details</summary>

- The legal entity and payment provider are on the Launch Coach roadmap in socialus-legal.
- For counsel, as pointers only (detail lives in socialus-legal): holding pledges and paying contributors; whether a Wonder needs a fiscal host; the role contract and IP licence template; sales tax and import duty on group orders; refunds and buyer protection; that buying is never investing (no returns, shares or payouts beyond a refund of unused reserve).

</details>
