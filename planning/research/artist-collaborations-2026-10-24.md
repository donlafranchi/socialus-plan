# Artist collaborations on Pages

**As of 2026-10-24** · Source: Don's idea, 2026-10-24 · Path: new territory (image rights and member trust; the pilot touches who may post on a Page).

**Bottom line: yes, but as original commissioned art, not fan art.** A Page owner invites a local artist; the artist makes a piece for that Page; the Page shows it with the artist's name and a link. Fan art is the wrong base: it copies someone else's trademarks, logos and player likenesses (the Kings, the NBA), which we won't host and the artist can't license.

**Your one call:** how much to build for a pilot. **Recommended: B**, an invite-and-consent flow with credit and a Page-only licence, no payment yet.

## Your words

"Is there a way for us to use fan art for photos? We could be helping local artists this way by allowing them to create and post images of their art on certain pages if the page owner and artist collaborate?"

## The picture

An owner invites an artist. The artist says yes and uploads the piece, choosing to let that Page show it. The Page shows the art with "Art by <name>" linking to the artist's own Page or profile. Neighbours who like it follow the artist. The owner can take it down at any time; so can the artist.

## A, B or C

- **A) Credit line only.** The owner uploads the artist's work with a credit and link by hand. Nothing new to build; relies on trust, and the artist's consent lives in a chat, not the product.
- **B) Invite and consent (recommended).** The owner invites a named artist; the artist accepts and uploads, granting a licence to show it on that Page only, revocable. Credit and link are automatic. Small build: one invitation, one consent record, one credit line. No money.
- **C) B plus payment.** The owner pays the artist through the member side. Most useful to artists, but it touches money and counsel, and members are the investors and the only people paid out; hold it until B shows owners and artists want it.

**Recommendation: B.** It is the smallest thing that gets consent and credit right, which is what could go wrong. C waits on evidence.

### Precedents

- **Behance and Dribbble** credit the creator on every piece and link back to their profile; collaborations list each contributor. [Behance](https://www.behance.net/), [Dribbble](https://dribbble.com/)
- **Etsy and Society6** give artists a storefront; art appears on products with the artist named and a link to their shop. [Society6](https://society6.com/)
- **Threadless** runs on artists submitting original designs that the community votes on, the artist credited and paid per sale. [Threadless](https://www.threadless.com/)
- **Instagram collab posts** appear on both accounts, and only after the invited account accepts: the model for owner-invites, artist-consents. [Instagram Collabs](https://help.instagram.com/)
- **Spotify Canvas** shows the visual with the artist credited, supplied and approved by the artist, not by the platform. [Spotify for Artists](https://artists.spotify.com/)
- **Local mural programs** (city and arts-council commissions) pair a property owner with a named artist under a written agreement that sets credit, duration and who may reproduce the work. Sacramento's own arts commission runs one. Federal law (VARA) also gives murals' artists rights over destruction.

The common pattern: **credit travels with the work, the artist accepts before it appears, and the licence is narrow.**

### Credit, owner approval and artist consent

- **Owner approval:** only the Page's owner can invite an artist and place the art; the owner can remove it.
- **Artist consent:** the artist accepts the invitation and uploads the piece themselves. No one can post an artist's work for them.
- **Credit:** "Art by <name>", linked to the artist's Page or profile, shown wherever the art is shown on that Page.
- **Original only:** the artist confirms the piece is theirs, with no third-party trademarks, logos or player likenesses. Team and brand names in text are fine. Reports on a piece go through the normal reporting path.

### Image rights: the licence the artist grants

- **Scope:** display on that one Page (and its cards in Explore), nothing else; no resale, no use by SocialUs elsewhere.
- **Revocable:** the artist can withdraw it, and the Page stops showing it.
- **Ownership:** the artist keeps the copyright. The words of the licence are for counsel to check before launch, not for this note to settle.
- **Photos of a mural:** a photo of a mural on a building is the artist's work too; same consent and credit apply.

### Payment, optional and later

If an owner wants to pay an artist, it goes through the member side, where members are the investors and the only people paid out. SocialUs takes transaction income. Anything that moves money goes to counsel first. B does not need it; C adds it.

### The smallest pilot

Two or three local artists matched with two or three Page owners who each want a piece (for example a cafe wanting a banner, a market wanting a poster, a maker wanting product art). Four weeks. Plain agreement each time (credit, Page-only, revocable). Success: each Page shows the piece with the credit, the artist gets at least a few new followers, and no one asks to withdraw. Find the artists through the owners and the dark-site outreach list.

### Open

- Where the artist's credit links: their own Page or their own site.
- Whether an artist needs a Page to be credited, or a name and link is enough for the pilot.
