## 2. The Page-rule conflict: A or B

**Recommendation: B, done as "draft first, publish on yes".** We prepare the Page privately from the business's own public listing; the postcard's QR shows the owner that draft; one tap ("Yes, put us on SocialUs") publishes it. Nothing about a business that never said yes is ever public. Path: new territory (it touches the #353/#517 source ruling and member trust).

| | A: stub unclaimed Page now | B: outreach first, Page on yes (recommended) |
|---|---|---|
| Owner effort | Scan → see Page → Claim (a request today) | Scan → see draft → tap Yes (one step) |
| Fits #353/#517 source rule | **No.** Needs a new source kind ("public listing") | **Yes.** Source = "the owner said yes", same as #521 |
| Where the data comes from | Google/Yelp are off-limits (THEMES rule 6, and their terms); only county ag / market vendor lists remain | Same lists, but only for our private draft and the address on the card |
| Trust risk | A business that chose to stay offline finds itself listed without asking | None: we asked first |
| Reach | Every dark business is findable at once | Only the ones who say yes |
| Opt-out | Needed for listed Pages and for never-listed ones | Needed only so we never mail twice |
| Build | New stub Page kind, new source label | Reuses #521's "owner's recorded yes" path + a claim code |
| Fits "build the system, then get out of the way" | Partly: still needs a staff check on each claim today | **Yes**, if the postcard code is the proof (below) |

<details><summary>Precedents</summary>

- **A is how Yelp and Google work:** both list businesses from third-party data and invite owners to claim ([Yelp for Business](https://biz.yelp.com/), [Google Business Profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/2911778)). That is exactly the "listed without asking" feeling SocialUs's pitch sets itself against.
- **B is how LocalHarvest and Nextdoor work:** a farm or business listing exists because the owner created it ([LocalHarvest](https://www.localharvest.org/), [Nextdoor Business](https://business.nextdoor.com/)).
- **The postcard as proof is Google's:** Google Business Profile mails a postcard with a code to the business address; entering it verifies the owner with no staff ([Google: verify by postcard](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242)). We borrow the mechanism, not the listing model.
</details>

<details><summary>Why not A, in one more line</summary>

The only sources A could use without breaking THEMES rule 6 (never Yelp, Google or another directory) are county ag directories and market vendor lists, and those are directories too. A needs Don to change the #353 source ruling; B does not.
</details>

## 3. How claiming works today, and the QR flow designed against it

**Today a claim does not complete.** "Claim this Page" on an unclaimed Page records a request (name, contact, note) that an operator sees in Admin → Unclaimed Pages; no code hands the Page to the owner. **V** (`requestUnclaimedClaimAction` → `page_claim_requests`; nothing clears `unclaimed_at`). Removal does complete, instantly, signed in or out. **V**

**The flow to build (B + postcard code):**

1. We mail a card to the business's published address. It carries a QR to `socialus.org/hello/<code>`; the code is single-use and tied to one draft.
2. Scan → a phone page shows the draft Page (name, town, category, published phone; no photos) with two buttons: **Yes, put us on SocialUs** and **No thanks, don't contact us again**.
3. **Yes** → sign in with an email code (the existing sign-in) → the Page is published and owned by that member. The code is the proof: it reached the business's own mailbox. No staff step.
4. **No thanks** → the business goes on the do-not-contact list at once (below). No sign-in needed.
5. Ignore it → nothing is ever published; the draft is deleted after the pilot.
6. Disputes (two people claim one business, a wrong address) → staff, as today.

<details><summary>How each outreach channel fits "build the system, then get out of the way"</summary>

- **Postcard:** best fit. The card is both invitation and proof of ownership; the owner finishes alone.
- **In person:** good fit. Hand over the same card; the code still does the proving, the person only delivers it.
- **Phone call:** weaker. A yes on the phone has to be recorded by a person; we then mail the card anyway, or text a link (not allowed: no cold texts).
- **Contact form:** weak. Replies come back to a person, and dark sites' forms are often unwatched.
- **Self-serve by domain email** (proposed, not ruled) rarely helps here: dark-site businesses mostly use Gmail or no email. Phone-call verification works but needs the call path built; the postcard code covers them all.
</details>

## 4. Opt-out list (never re-added)

**A do-not-contact list the loader reads, so a business that says no is never mailed, called or given a Page again.**

- File: `scripts/unclaimed/opt-outs.json` in socialus-web, one row per business: name, town, the published phone or site it was matched on, date, channel it came through. No person's name.
- The loader (`load.ts` via `readPlanned`) drops any planned row that matches by site host, phone or name+town, and logs "held back: opted out". A test proves a matched row never loads.
- Also written to by: "No thanks" on the QR page, the existing removal button, and a person logging a phone or in-person no.
- Today the loader only skips Pages that already exist (hidden or not); a business that opted out before it had a Page, or whose Page was purged, would be re-added. **V** (`load.ts` header comment and query).
## 5. Outreach channels, ranked

**Primary: a mailed postcard to the published business address. Backup: a printed card handed over at the farm stand or market booth.** Phone is third, the site's contact form last.

| Rank | Channel | Effort for the business | Our cost | Fits self-serve | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Postcard** to published address | Scan, one tap | ~$1/card incl. postage (A) | Best: the card is the proof | Card lost in the pile; slow (5–10 days) |
| 2 | **In person**, card at stand or booth | Talk 1 minute, scan later | Someone's time, gas | Good: same card, same code | Needs a named person (Don's call); seasonal stands close in winter |
| 3 | **Call** the published phone | 30 seconds on the phone | Minutes | Weak: a person records the yes | High hang-up; feels like a sales call |
| 4 | **Contact form** on own site | Read an email | Minutes | Weak | Dark sites' forms often unwatched or broken |
| — | Cold texts, scraped emails, DMs, home addresses | — | — | — | **Not allowed** |

<details><summary>Why the postcard first</summary>

- It is the only channel that is local, asks nothing technical, works for businesses that don't read email, and carries its own proof of ownership.
- Google has trained small owners to expect a postcard with a code for exactly this ([Google: verify by postcard](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242)).
- For home-based bakers with no storefront, there is no published business address to mail; they are left out of the postcard pilot (see pilot list).
</details>

- 10 is too small for a rate. It shows whether owners scan at all and whether anyone objects.
</details>
