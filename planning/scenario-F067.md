## Story

Today "follower" and "member" mean the same thing in the code, and the PM wants the words in place before the behavior diverges. A customer who follows a bakery is called a follower; someone who joins a run club is called a member — same row, one new column, and today neither gains any access the other doesn't already have.

## Acceptance

1. **Privacy decides which relationship is written**: a private Page is joined as `relationship: 'member'`, anything else is followed as `relationship: 'follower'`. One query serves both. *(Amended 2026-09-15, Don. The original keyed this on `kind` — business = follower, group = member. Superseded: a bakery and a run club can both be open to anyone, and what differs is whether the thing is closed, not what sort of thing it is.)*
2. **The Page's owner can see who follows it, by name.** Nobody else can: not a member of the Page, not another follower, not a stranger (Don, 2026-09-30). *(Amended 2026-09-15, Don. The original read "never visible to anyone, including the business's own visitors, except a count where it earns its place" — that is superseded: knowing who follows you is the point of being followed.)*
3. A social group's current members are visible to each other; members who left are not.
4. Neither relationship affects any authorization or role check — both hold every guard rail F065 already established.

## Not this

Converting one relationship into the other. Any benefit members get that followers don't — this column makes that cheap later, it isn't this scenario.

## Rulings
- **2026-09-30 — Nobody sees who follows whom. A Page's owner sees who follows their Page, by name.** Don, on #246 and #248. The follow graph is readable by no member, signed in or out, except that a Page's owner reads their own Page's followers. `verbs.md`'s "numbers only" matrix row is corrected, and F067's open question on whether followers see each other is answered: they do not. - **2026-09-29 — A member is a more involved follower. The difference is involvement, not Page kind.** Don, ruling on `socialus-web` #248: *"A member is basically a more involved follower and a member probably has some reason to coordinate and schedule an RSVP is a follower is more passive. Just getting updates announcements but not needing to coordinate so perhaps businesses that sell products or services have followers and pages that are groups of people who do things are communities and those have members where you join an RSVP, etc. and I'm also thinking about Costco who has members and their business so I'm wondering if there's a way that we allow businesses to figure out creative ways to get members that are essentially more involved, but that's really distinction and it doesn't need to be more complicated than that for now"* **Reading (relayed with the ruling):** a **follower** is passive: they receive a Page's updates and announcements and coordinate nothing. A **member** is a more involved follower, with a reason to coordinate and schedule: joining, RSVPing, doing things with others. A member gets everything a follower does. **Page kind sets the default offer, not the rule:** a business selling products or services will typically have followers, and a Page that is a group of people doing things is a community with members, but **no Page kind is barred from having members** (the Costco point). **A community is a Page's members, not its followers.** **Deliberately no more complicated than that for now:** no taxonomy is built on top of it. **Approval, ruled the same day: the Page decides whether membership needs approval.** Today that is a plain yes/no by whoever runs the Page, per request, **with no criteria attached**. It uses the existing `group_memberships.confirmed_by_member_id` / `confirmed_at`. **Join criteria are deferred:** questions, conditions or anything else an owner sets to gate entry. Don: *"in Facebook members groups there are certain criteria for becoming a member that people can set and we should probably have something like that but not yet."* Not in scope, not built, not designed, and **no hook for it goes into the approval work**: no criteria column, no empty questions table, no placeholder in the request flow. **Taken up when** a Page that requires approval asks to screen requests by something other than a yes/no (a runner asking for join questions, or reporting that they approve against conditions kept somewhere else), or when Don raises it, whichever comes first. The two relationships no longer carry identical rights. [replaces 2026-09-08: with identical rights today] [binds tiers=planning,code surfaces=pages,membership,announcements]
- **2026-09-15 — Privacy decides the relationship: private means membership, not following.** Don: *"Anything private wouldn't have followers, they have members because you can't follow something. You'd have to become a member in order to see what's in it."* **Generalised: the relationship follows from the Page's `discoverability`, not its Page kind** — `private` gives membership only, `listed` and `unlisted` both allow following. **Not a special case for family Pages**; family is caught by it because its trigger defaults it private. **It corrects the tools mapping**, which assigned following to the four community kinds unconditionally — they default to `listed` but can be set private, and following must stop when they are. Home: `product/foundation/verbs.md` § Privacy decides the relationship. F067's single `relationship` column is the schema.
- **2026-09-15 — The baseline ruling needs one correction, flagged not applied: "announcing and a following list" makes no sense for a family Page.** It defaults to private, so a Page follower on it is meaningless or a privacy hole. **Family joins, it is not followed.** The baseline should read **"announcing, and either following or joining"** — F067 already separates the two at the schema level with one `relationship` column, and every discussion so far has treated them as one thing.
- **2026-09-08 — A follower of a business Page never sees other followers, and the Page never shows its follower list publicly — to anyone, including anonymous visitors.** A follower list on a business is a customer list, published; the business may see its own audience, nobody else has a reason to. A social group's members list is a different rule for a different noun — members may see each other. [always]
- **2026-09-08 — A follower and a member are the same row (`relationship: 'member' | 'follower'`, defaulted from Page kind).** The column exists so the two can diverge later without a migration — interest and belonging are different relationships and must not share a row, or the weaker claim inherits the stronger one's access.
