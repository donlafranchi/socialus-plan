## Story

Lupe's bakery is in Granite Bay. In her Page's settings a line asks where the Page's give-back goes: Granite Bay, read from her address, or another town she types. She picks Granite Bay, and the Page may carry the local badge. A chain's Page in Roseville sends its give-back to its head-office town: it is listed like any other Page and carries no badge. A visitor from Davis sees Lupe's badge and reads it as: money spent here stays here.

## Acceptance

1. **A Page has a home town: the town of its anchor Location's place**, or its owner's home town when it has no Location (F103 criterion 5). What the Page shows about where it is does not change.
2. **A Page owner declares one give-back town: the home town, or any town in the place tree.** Until the owner declares, nothing is declared: the Page is not local and carries no badge. No default is written on the owner's behalf.
3. **The Page is local when its give-back town is its home town, and only then may the local badge show.** The badge says it is the owner's own declaration (2026-09-30, "Says locally owned" style; words to the PM). A give-back elsewhere means no badge and no negative mark.
4. **The badge reads the same from every vantage** (F105): it is about the seller's town, never the viewer's.
5. **The owner can change the declaration at any time while no money flows.** The yearly lock and which fund inside the town receives it arrive with funds, not here.

## Not this

Funds, payouts, fees or a ledger (Later; `contributions.md`). The Locally owned residence fact and its evidence ladder (`business-jurisdiction.md`, decided with the badge design). Verifying the declaration. A badge in beta (cut 2026-10-06). Choosing a neighbourhood, county or free text as the destination.

## Why

- **Lives somewhere better?** No: where a seller's give-back goes exists nowhere else.
- **Only locals know?** Yes: which businesses keep money in town is exactly what a local wants to know and cannot find.
- **Gets people together?** Indirectly: it tells a finder which doors to walk through.
- **Stays fresh on its own?** Yes: a declaration is one fact that changes rarely and is true until the owner changes it.
- **Who keeps it current?** The owner, once; the platform never re-derives it.
- **Better as more people join?** Yes: the receipts add up to a town's own picture of where its money goes.

**Path: new territory, ruled** (ruling 2026-10-11). The town is the unit because the ruling says "the town where the business is"; what a *community* is for funds stays the open question in `places.md`, since it bites only when money flows. The 2026-10-04 ruling that the badge's area is the whole MSA is superseded. No default: nobody is called local without saying so, the same principle as the owner's own claim (2026-09-30) and Etsy's seller-declared "handmade".

**Precedents.** Etsy's seller declarations (handmade, vintage) are the seller's word and say so; B Corp and 1% for the Planet are declared commitments with a public mark; Nextdoor's "Local business" is a claim, not a verification. Conflict: none; all three badge a declared commitment and leave verification to evidence later.

## Rulings
- **2026-10-11 — Local means money comes back to the community where it was generated. A Page owner chooses where their give-back goes; an owner who sends it to the town where the business is counts as local and may carry the local badge, and an owner who sends it anywhere else is not a local business and gets no badge. A consumer can see where their money goes when they spend it; that is the purpose of an anti-extraction local discovery app.** Don.
