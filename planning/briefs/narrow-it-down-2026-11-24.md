# Get specific: taste as specific as the member

**As of 2026-11-24** · Source: the PM's words below; the recommendations brief (2026-11-18) and its 2026-11-20 ruling in F113; F092 (narrowing writes what it did), item tags (#1318), F136 (hyper-specific search), F121 (shelves); the precedents linked below · Maintained by: hand (brief; deleted at 30 days or once ruled and placed)

*Earlier, 2026-11-21, on "I went":* "this isn't the right kind of feedback. It's one small piece, but I think there's better ways than this." Selling, teaching, trading and doing are most of what SocialUs offers; events are one kind.

**Bottom line (updated 2026-11-25): recommend at the level a member actually cares about. "Bicycling" is too broad; "steel single speed, hardtail mountain bike, beach cruiser" is the taste. Describe everything in facets; put one "More like this" tap on every card that the member never has to manage, kept quietly and fading as tastes move; and once enough taps agree, show the member a short summary of their taste that they confirm or fix. Start there, ask beta members whether the summary is them, and add the rest in order as the numbers say it works.**

## The theme: four parts

**1. Everything is described in facets** (what we know about a Page, post or listing)
- A facet is one plain fact about the thing. For a bike sale: *bicycle › single speed · steel frame · used · $300–500 · Midtown · a neighbor selling*. For a class: *ceramics › wheel throwing · beginner · evenings · 6 weeks · a maker teaching*.
- Every offer gets the same few kinds of facet: **what** (a tree, broad to narrow), **made of / style**, **way** (sell, trade, teach, gather, repair, lend), **new, used or handmade**, **price band**, **when**, **where**, **who** (neighbor, maker, shop, group).
- **SocialUs fills them in,** one small model call when something is posted, reading its words and photos; the owner sees them and corrects any. Members never type tags (2026-10-12 ruling); posters do no extra work.
- This grows from item tags, already ruled to lead search (#1318): tags become the "what" tree, and the other facets sit beside it.
- Precedents: [eBay item specifics](https://www.ebay.com/sellercenter/listings/item-specifics) (brand, size, style: the facts buyers filter by, set per category), [Etsy attributes](https://help.etsy.com/hc/articles/115014502508) (material, style, occasion, varying by category), Spotify's thousands of micro-genres ([Every Noise at Once](https://everynoise.com/)) — taste lives far below "rock".

**2. Narrow it down, in the conversation**
- A member asks; the answer comes back as a short list of cards. They narrow in words ("steel only", "not road bikes", "cheaper") or by marking the good ones.
- Each narrowing is a facet choice, so it is precise and it carries the why. This is a well-studied shape: critiquing recommenders, where people refine by reacting to examples ("like this, but cheaper") ([Chen & Pu, survey, 2012](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/umuai/ChenP12)).
- It already matches F092's rule: narrowing writes what it did, in words.

**3. Mark the good ones: the button you asked about**
- On each card in an answer: **"More like this"** (a small up-arrow or "+"), and **"Not this"**.
- Tapping "More like this" on one or more cards narrows the next answer **now**, in this conversation, to what the marked cards share, and says so: *"Narrowing to: steel frame · single speed. Change"*. The member can tap a facet to drop it.
- **Why they chose them** is worked out from the facets: what the marked cards share that the unmarked ones in the same answer don't. Two steel single speeds marked, a carbon road bike and an e-bike left: the why is *steel, single speed*. When it's unclear, the assistant asks once: "Was it the steel frame or the single speed?"
- **Not saved by default.** It lives for the conversation. Only when the same narrowing comes back in three separate conversations does the assistant ask: *"You keep looking for steel single speeds. Keep an eye out for them?"* A yes writes it into the taste record; a no or silence lets it go.
- Precedents: Google News' "fewer stories like this" and like controls ([help](https://support.google.com/googlenews/answer/9010862)), Pinterest's "More ideas like this" ([help](https://help.pinterest.com/en/article/tune-your-home-feed)), Netflix and Spotify thumbs that tune now without a list to manage.

**4. A taste record as specific as the member**
- Interests are a tree, not a list: **Bicycling › steel single speed · hardtail mountain bike · beach cruiser**. The broad one holds the narrow ones.
- Each line shows its reason ("you kept this 2026-12-02 · 4 conversations") and can be edited, narrowed, widened or deleted on its own, in You and by asking the assistant ("forget beach cruisers").
- What feeds it is the 2026-11-20 list, made general: asks, confirmed narrowings, saves, follows, Remind me, the deliberate act each kind of offer has (messaged the seller, signed up for the class, "I'm coming", bought, traded), and "Not this". "How was it?" (Loved it / It was OK / Not for me / leave feedback) is one small extra, asked only after something with a clear end.

## The button: decided 2026-11-25

*The PM:* "as far as the button I want the method to be adaptable over time. one time is not enough. the button creates a taste profile. the taste profile is more like a summary once there is enough to go on. I essentially want the likes or "show me more like this" button to be something the user doesn't need to manage and perhaps is ephemeral and always evolving. and to answer the question I think we need user feedback to decide. what is the best first option and then how to expand? what gives us more data to provide better results?"

- **The tap:** "More like this" on every card (Explore, search, chat answers). It narrows the answer in front of the member now, and is kept quietly as a fading signal; there is no list of taps to manage.
- **Fading follows the member:** a tap fades unless more taps agree; a facet tapped again and again over weeks strengthens, one that stops fades out (the "always loved / on occasion / passing spike" patterns in the taste research). Raw taps expire on F113's schedule.
- **The summary is what the member sees:** once enough taps agree, You shows "Seems you're into: steel single speeds · hardtail mountain bikes · beach cruisers", each line with "That's me" or "Not really". The summary leans what they're shown; a line they drop isn't guessed back.
- **Precedents for taps you never manage:** Instagram's and TikTok's likes tune the feed without a list to tend; [Spotify's taste profile](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2023/10/exclude-from-your-taste-profile) builds silently and lets you exclude; Netflix thumbs ([Netflix, 2017](https://about.netflix.com/en/news/goodbye-stars-hello-thumbs)).

## What gives the most data

- **Most per member, soonest:** the ask itself, then taps kept over time. Keeping taps (what you chose) gives far more than taps that vanish with the conversation, which gave us almost nothing to learn from.
- **Most per tap:** a tap on a card with good facets, read against the other cards in the same answer. Without facets a tap says "this one"; with them it says "steel, single speed, used". So facets come first.
- **Most trustworthy:** acts that cost the member something: messaging a seller, signing up, buying, "I'm coming". Fewer of these, but they confirm what the taps suggest.
- **Least per member, most across members:** similar-member patterns. Useful only once a market has enough members; that is the last step, not the first.

## First slice, and how it grows

**First (recommended):**
1. **Facets on every Page, post and listing,** filled in by SocialUs when it's posted, correctable by the owner. Nothing else works well without them.
2. **"More like this" on every card,** one tap, kept quietly, fading.
3. **The summary in You** once enough taps agree, with "That's me" / "Not really".
- **Member feedback that decides the next step:** the share of summary lines marked "That's me", and how often a tapped answer leads to an act (saved, messaged, signed up, bought). Read weekly in beta.

**Then, each only when the one before shows it works:**
2. **"Not this"** on cards, and a one-line "Was it the steel or the single speed?" when the why is unclear.
3. **The assistant uses the summary** in chat answers and says why ("because you're into single speeds").
4. **"How was it?"** (Loved it / It was OK / Not for me / leave feedback) after a class, a purchase or a gathering.
5. **Similar members,** per market, when it beats the summary on the same measures with ten or more members behind each pattern.

**Alternatives for the first slice:**
- **B. Skip the summary at first:** taps tune results silently; add the summary later. Fastest to build, but no "That's me" feedback, which is the test you asked for.
- **C. Start with the assistant asking:** "What kind of bikes?" after an ask, no tap yet. Rich words, but it adds a question to every answer, and the data stops when the member stops answering.

## How it would be measured

- **Narrowing works:** after a member marks good ones, how often the next answer has one they save, message about, sign up for or buy, against the first answer.
- **Fewer rounds:** the number of narrowings before an act, falling over a member's first months.
- **The why is right:** how often a member drops a facet from "Narrowing to:"; a high rate means the deduction is wrong.
- **The summary is them:** the share of summary lines marked "That's me"; a low rate means it shows too early or the facets are wrong.
- **Never counted:** time in the app, views, scrolling.
