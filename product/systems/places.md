# Places

A place is a recognized geographic scope — region, state, county, city, neighborhood — that everything else anchors to: a Location sits inside a place, a Page carries a place anchor, an Item inherits one, URLs nest under the place tree. Places answer two structural questions: where does a URL belong, and what counts as "near me."

**Places are platform-curated, not user-created — deliberately.** A "city" is a unit of recognized civic geography; if Members could declare one, someone could declare their block a city and own the URL namespace forever. User-declared geographic scope has its own primitive (`locations.kind='area'`) for service areas and custom polygons that aren't infrastructural. Curation by the platform is what keeps the URL hierarchy and the locality index trustworthy rather than a land-grab or a popularity contest.

**Deliberately separate from Locations.** A Location is a specific point a Member declared (Drake's Bar, at its actual coordinates); a place is an infrastructural scope nobody declares (the neighborhood it's in). Conflating them either gives every Location curation authority it shouldn't have, or makes places user-declarable and destroys the URL namespace's stability.

**Parent-scoped slug uniqueness.** A place's slug is unique under its parent, not globally — two distinct Oak Parks (a Sacramento neighborhood, an Illinois city) are two rows with different parents and neither needs a name-mangling suffix. The hierarchy carries the disambiguation so the slug stays legible.

**Granularities can be skipped.** A small town's city row can parent directly to state; some neighborhoods have no universally-recognized boundary and never get their own row — their Locations anchor to the parent city. The hierarchy is variable-depth by design, not every place uses every level.

**Distinct from the business-jurisdiction ladder.** Places are a discovery/URL surface; jurisdiction (`business-jurisdiction.md`) is an evidence surface using ZIPs, not place rows. A business Group carries both a place anchor (for its URL) and a jurisdiction tier (for its badge) — never one standing in for the other.

## The three rings replace the metro

*Approved 2026-10-11 (ruling; F103 the market and the zip gate, F104 the scope and order, F105 the vantage, F106 the give-back town and the local badge). The code reads the metro until the order of work in `planning/research/local-inventory-2026-10-11.md` § 4 runs: the gate before the freeze, the feed and the vantage in the first fortnight after beta, the badge when badges return.*

**The rings are the member's own, built from the place tree and centred on their home.** Three of them, coarse to fine:

| Ring | Unit | Rows | Who defines it | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hood | neighbourhood, where one is recognised | `kind=neighborhood` | platform-curated, as today; turns on in a town once owners there pick neighbourhoods | the finest ordering band; never a scope on its own |
| town | a Census Place (incorporated city or town, or a Census Designated Place); the county remainder where there is none; a tribal area only if the member chose it | `kind=city` (a CDP is a city row) and county rows | Census TIGER, imported; nobody draws one | the **home community**: where a seller's give-back goes and the unit a fund belongs to |
| area | a named set of towns and county subdivisions, usually whole counties minus the parts that are another market | `kind=region` with county and county-subdivision children | the operator adds a county with one row and subtracts a subdivision with one row; the Census draws the subdivisions (County Subdivisions, CCDs in California [A: exact names unverified]) | the **market**: who may sign up, what Explore scopes to, what opens and closes |

The MSA/CSA becomes a code on the area row, read by reports and nothing member-facing. The metro overlay (`metro_polygons`) retires once every reader has moved to the area row; what it did survives in the tree.

**Order, not distance.** Every surface that scopes by locality scopes to the member's area and orders hood → town → area → online. Inside the area, towns are ordered by **adjacency**: the towns that touch yours first (Census Place polygons, `ST_Touches`, county remainders as the glue), then the towns that touch those, and so on. It is a count of towns crossed, never a mile; nothing shows it, it only orders. No miles are shown or filtered anywhere. Widening past the area (a neighbouring area, the state) happens only in its own labelled section, never silently. Rings sized by density, drive time or behaviour are later and data-led; the model needs none of that to start.

**Why counties are not enough.** Placer County runs from Roseville up I-80 to the Tahoe north shore; El Dorado County from El Dorado Hills to South Lake Tahoe. The valley and foothill parts are the Sacramento market; the Tahoe basin is not, and Tahoe City and South Lake Tahoe are not one community with Granite Bay or Placerville. So a market is built from county subdivisions, not counties: the Sacramento area is Sacramento and Yolo counties whole, plus Placer minus its Tahoe subdivision, plus El Dorado minus its South Lake Tahoe subdivision. The Tahoe area is those two subdivisions plus Truckee (Nevada County) and the Nevada shore: a market may cross a state line, because a state line is a political boundary, not a physical one (the PM, 2026-10-12, F103). Each opens on its own sign-ups. Signup resolves zip → town or subdivision → area through the Census ZCTA relationship files, which are free [A].

**A market opens on sign-ups, by a person.** Makers and finders (people who come to find things; the waitlist calls them patrons) sign up before the market opens. It opens when both counts clear their per-market thresholds (F076: 50/250 to start, tunable), the operator can afford it, and a person flips it. Sacramento is staff-opened; every market after is community-led this way. A maker in a closed market can build a Page that is absent from Explore until the day the market opens.

**What "local" means to a seller.** The PM's definition, 2026-10-11: *local means money comes back to the community where it was generated.* A seller's give-back goes where the seller chooses. If it goes to the **town where the business is** (the Place its anchor Location sits in), the business is local and may carry the local badge; if it goes anywhere else, the business is not local and gets no badge. So the badge is the receipt for the choice, and a consumer reads it as "my money stays here". For a visitor looking from a vantage away from home, the badge is how they choose to support the community they are visiting (Don, 2026-10-11). Discovery is wider than money: a seller is *found* across their area and *local* to their town. When badges return, the local badge is the receipt for the give-back town (F106). Locally owned, the owner-side claim, is a circulation question the owner first makes in their own words, and how near is near is an open question, in `business-jurisdiction.md` (the PM, 2026-10-12); the ladder itself is unchanged, with the market in place of the MSA.

**Community, and where money goes.** "Community" is a loose word: people who come together for a common purpose, and a community can have a Page like any other. Money is not loose: give-back goes only to a legal entity the community forms, tied to its Page (see Rulings). The product needs only that tie; structure and tax are for `socialus-legal`.

**The rings are a starting point, not a fence: the vantage moves (F105).** Stress-tested in four other places, dense and sparse: `planning/research/rings-stress-test-2026-10-11.md`; where "local" is defined and read today: `planning/research/local-inventory-2026-10-11.md`. The place a member looks from is their **vantage**. By default it is their home town, and the rings centre there. A member who is going to South Lake Tahoe for the day types it into search, and for that moment the vantage is South Lake Tahoe: Home and Explore re-centre, the rings rebuild around it (its hood if any, its town, its area), and the order is hood → town → area from *there*. Nothing about the member changes: home stays home, the give-back rules read the seller's town, not the viewer's vantage, and a vantage is session state, not a stored travel plan. A vantage in a closed market shows what is waiting there and what ships online, not Pages that are hidden until the market opens. A vantage in another open area shows that area in full. **A visitor with no location is prompted to enter a location or choose a market, with an arrow pointing to the market selector (F105).** Precedents: Yelp and Google Maps search "near" a typed place with current location as the default; Airbnb and Eventbrite put "Where" beside "What"; Nextdoor locks a member to home and lets them see nothing else. Conflict: Nextdoor's lock is right for money and wrong for looking; the vantage follows Yelp for looking and Nextdoor for home.

**Colloquial places.** Apple Hill is a growers' association of about fifty farms around Camino and Placerville, not a Census Place. Such a place is a `region` row *below* a county, platform-added like any place, so a Page can say it is at Apple Hill and a finder can browse it; it is never a home community, because money needs a unit the Census and a bank both recognise. The same shape serves "Tahoe-Truckee" or "Gold Country" above the county, which may now cross a state line (F103).

**Worked example: Apple Hill, from Sacramento.** A member living in Sacramento city has hood = their neighbourhood, town = Sacramento, area = Sacramento (Sacramento, Placer, El Dorado and Yolo counties). Camino is a CDP in El Dorado County, so an Apple Hill farm is inside their area ring: it appears in their Explore after hood and town content, labelled by its town ("Camino") or its colloquial place ("Apple Hill"), never "45 minutes" or "40 miles". The farm's own rings: town = Camino, area = Sacramento. Its give-back goes to Camino's fund, or, if Camino has none, accrues as pending or goes to a fund its owner designated under the rule below. A Bay Area member, outside the area, does not see the farm as local; they see it only in a labelled wider section once their own area is open, or by its link.

**Worked example: zip 95746, Granite Bay.** A CDP in Placer County on Folsom Lake, touching the Sacramento County line. **Hood** none yet. **Town** Granite Bay: the home community, where a Granite Bay seller's give-back goes. **Area** Sacramento, ordered by adjacency: first the touching towns (Roseville, Loomis, Folsom, Orangevale [A: verify against TIGER polygons]), across the county line unnoticed; then Rocklin, Citrus Heights, Fair Oaks; then Auburn, Lincoln, Rancho Cordova; downtown Sacramento, Elk Grove, Davis, then Placerville and Camino. Tahoe City, Kings Beach and Truckee are in the Tahoe area, which opens on its own sign-ups; a Granite Bay member sees them only in a labelled wider section, if ever.

**Precedents.** Craigslist's hand-drawn regions give every address a home and use colloquial names; Nextdoor's neighbourhood is the finest ring and "Nearby Neighborhoods" the next, with a founding-member gate before a neighbourhood opens; the USDA local food directories key on city and county, not metro. Conflict: Craigslist's unit is the region, Nextdoor's the neighbourhood; here the area is the market and the hood the finest ring, so both fit.

[open-question owner=don raised=2026-10-12] How does a Page's give-back town (F106) relate to a community's legal entity? The town ring was how a fund was located; the recipient is now an entity. A) **The entity declares the town or towns it serves, and a Page's give-back town resolves to the entity that serves it** (recommended: the local badge stays a receipt for a town, and the money still lands in a legal entity). B) A Page owner picks an entity directly, and the badge reads the entity's declared town. C) One entity per town ring, no overlap: simplest to audit, hardest for a community that spans two towns. Tax-efficient structure for the entity is for counsel, not this question.

Because people act through Pages, so a place page is an index and a shared scoreboard, never a home for content. `socialus-web` #421. Rationale: `planning/research/url-plan-2026-10-06.md` §4, §7.4. [for now] [binds tiers=planning,code surfaces=place-url,explore]
- **2026-09-21 — A Page's canonical URL has no geography in it, and no member derivable from it.** Don's ruling. **No geography**, because metros today and neighbourhoods later must both leave the address untouched. **No member identity**, which he set as a safety requirement rather than a tidiness one: *"I want to keep members safe from people with bad intentions."* **State, city, metro and neighbourhood become indexes that forward to the canonical URL**, never alternative homes for it. **A metro ID in the address was considered and rejected** — it reintroduces the geographic coupling just refused, it does not separate two same-named Pages inside one metro, and it moves an address when a business relocates or a boundary is redrawn. **Same-name disambiguation is display**: *"Joe's Pizza — Davis"* in results and lens rows. **This resolves rather than collides with `verbs.md`'s refusal to rename an active slug** — that refusal exists because the slug is currently the address; once the ID carries identity, an address never moves (2026-10-06: the slug is frozen), so the rule is amended to *an address may not move* rather than deleted. Full record, the ID specification, the slug rules and the identity audit: `planning/URL-IDENTITY.md`. **Unblocks `socialus-web` #175.** [always] [binds tiers=planning,code surfaces=page-url]
- **2026-09-21 — One canonical address per Page, many indexes onto it. The Place in a URL is never part of a Page's identity.** Don asked whether a Page can live at two endpoints and whether things can be organised several ways at once. **Yes to many organisations, no to many addresses**, and the split is already the shape of `browse_feed`: metro, category, newest and who-you-follow are **lens arguments to one query**, not alternative homes. Many paths *to* a thing, one path *of* it. Built in `socialus-web` #175. [always] [binds tiers=planning,code surfaces=page-url]
  **What the repo already assumes, checked rather than inferred.** `groups.slug` is `not null unique` **globally**, and `/p/[...slug]` resolves a Page with `resolveShop(supabase, groupSplit.groupSlug)` — **by slug alone.** The place segments are split off and **never validated against the Page**. So `/p/ca/sacramento/g/mayas-bakery` and `/p/ny/albany/g/mayas-bakery` render the same Page **today**. **Many addresses already exist, unintentionally, and nothing canonicalises or redirects** — which is a duplicate-address defect on the live site, not a hypothetical.
  **Therefore the second option, and it needs no migration to be true:** the canonical address is `groups.slug`. The place prefix is a **courtesy segment**, and a non-canonical one **redirects to the canonical form rather than rendering a second copy**. It may never be promoted into identity — no place-scoped slug uniqueness, no compound key.
  **This is also the only reading compatible with a rule already on the books.** `verbs.md` forbids renaming an active Page's slug: *"the address doesn't follow it — a moved public URL is a broken link someone already shared."* **A Place derived at read time moves when a boundary is edited, a better-fitting polygon lands, or an anchor is corrected.** Putting a resolved value into the address would move addresses on a schedule nobody controls, which is the thing that rule exists to prevent.
  **T143 is not in tension with this, because T143 never contemplated the URL.** Stated plainly rather than stretched: `resolvePagePlacements` returns `{source, kind, label, lng, lat}` and is rendered as a *"where you are now"* line on the Page. It is about **displaying** where a Page is. **Resolved-not-stored governs where a Page appears; it does not govern what a Page's address is**, and reading it as though it did is how the #175 fix would have quietly made every shared link perishable.
- **2026-09-30 — No distance is shown anywhere.** Don, on F094. The venue page's "X mi away" goes, with nothing in its place. **A Page has a location, or says where it usually shows up** (which farmers market, say). **A Page with no address is in the Pages directory and not on the map.** [for now] [binds tiers=planning,code surfaces=venue,map,pages]
- **2026-10-11 — The MSA is replaced. A market is a named set of whole counties and county subdivisions (a `region` row in the place tree); the zip decides a person's market and home town; Explore scopes to the vantage's market and orders hood → town → market by town adjacency → online, never by distance; a member may look from any typed town for the session, home by default; and a Page is local when its declared give-back town is its own town. Approved for build as F103–F106.** The PM, 2026-10-11, confirming the morning's direction that the metro rulings were scaffolding. Because a county line is the wrong edge for Tahoe and the MSA the wrong edge for Yuba-Sutter, and the rings expand where a metro cannot. Markets still open on sign-ups by a person's act (F076, mechanism unchanged); "local means where a thing is, not who may see it" (2026-09-30) and "no distance anywhere" stand; the zip is kept and nobody picks (2026-09-30), now literally.
